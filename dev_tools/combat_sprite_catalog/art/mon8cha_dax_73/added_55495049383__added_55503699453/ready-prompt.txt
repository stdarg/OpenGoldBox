OpenGold combat sprite specification: bipedal-v1 (2026-10-17)

Create one independently designed full-body monster combat sprite in crisp pixel
art. Use the monster's current text description and the selected equipment set.
Before every generation or regeneration, the exact full prompt sent to the
image generator must be saved in that monster's description under an IMAGEGEN
PROMPT heading. If this text changes for a retry, update and save the monster
description before making the next request; prompt files alone are insufficient.
No original game image, extracted sprite, or original-art contact sheet may be an
input reference. Do not copy the pose-guide mannequin's identity or clothes.
The saved sprite_size comes from the game's decoded combat-art dimensions,
measured in 24-pixel source tiles; never replace it with an SRD creature-space
category. Do not use original game artwork as a generation reference.

FACING: Front three-quarter view toward the RIGHT EDGE OF THE IMAGE (screen
right), approximately 45 degrees between frontal and right-side profile. The
chest/front of the body remains partly visible. Keep head, torso and feet oriented
consistently. Preserve this view in BOTH frames; do not turn away into a rear view
or full side profile during the action. The attack travels toward screen right.

MELEE READY: Hold the one-handed or two-handed weapon UP in a combat guard,
poised to strike. It must not dangle at rest. One-handed weapons have one hand on
the grip; two-handed weapons have both hands visibly and plausibly on the grip
or shaft. A thrusting weapon can be held up and aimed forward, ready to thrust.
MELEE ACTION: Show the weapon near the END of a strike: a late swing or a nearly
completed thrust appropriate to the weapon. Show a clear change from ready, not
another guard or windup. Maintain the appropriate grip through the strike.

PROJECTILE READY: Hold the weapon loaded and ready to fire: arrow nocked on bow,
bolt loaded on crossbow, or stone seated in sling pouch. This is before release.
PROJECTILE ACTION: The instant just AFTER the arrow, bolt, stone or other
projectile has been released toward screen right, with appropriate follow-through.
The projectile is no longer nocked, loaded or in the sling pouch; it may already
be outside the image. Keep the bow, crossbow or sling in the character's hands.
For a thrown weapon, ready holds it poised to throw; action is just after release
and that thrown weapon is no longer in the hand. The SAME arm that holds the
weapon in ready must follow through in action. Trace its hand back to its
shoulder in both frames; do not extend the offhand while leaving the throwing
arm bent behind the head or resting at the waist. An empty extended hand is
not enough unless it belongs to the former weapon arm. Do not turn a wand into
a bow.
THROWN-WEAPON ARM CONTINUITY: Before drawing action, identify the arm that
actually grips the thrown object in ready by tracing its shoulder, elbow,
wrist, and hand. If the object has a two-handed grip, identify the primary
throwing arm and the supporting arm. In action, that SAME throwing arm follows
through toward screen right: its empty hand extends past the torso in the
attack direction, near shoulder height or higher. The other arm cannot
substitute for it. A lowered throwing hand is a failed throw even if the
object has disappeared or the other hand points right. Keep the shoulder,
elbow, wrist, and open hand visibly connected; do not detach the arm or move
the whole figure to fit its reach.
BARE-HANDED ATTACKS BY MONSTERS WITH ARMS: Before generating or regenerating
the attack, ask the user to choose PUNCH or TWO-ARM FORWARD REACH for the exact monster
and variant, unless that choice was already explicitly supplied by the user.
Do not infer the choice from humanoid shape, a saved description, or earlier
art. In EITHER ACTION, lean the UPPER BODY toward screen right into the attack.
For PUNCH, extend the striking fist forward toward the target; the action must
read as a forceful punch, not an idle reach. For TWO-ARM FORWARD REACH, extend
BOTH ARMS FORWARD toward screen right with both hands outstretched.
In READY, hold the arms poised for the chosen attack. Keep the planted feet at
their exact ready-frame coordinates and preserve the body's scale and facing.
If a magical or other unusual attack is unspecified, ask for its ready/action
behavior before generating.

FOUR-LEGGED ANIMAL BITES: In ACTION, lunge the head and neck toward screen right
with the mouth OPEN WIDE. Keep the planted paws, rear body, camera, and scale
registered to READY. Animate the upper body into the bite; do not slide or
recenter the entire animal. Apply the same bite motion to the six-legged
Displacer Beast while preserving all six legs and both shoulder tentacles.

EQUIPMENT: Draw only the selected weapon/shield setup, plus appropriate other
gear from the record. Retain the shield in both frames when the set includes it.
For a spellcaster whose saved wand is classified as gear, show that wand in the
default casting pair without changing its equipment classification.
Do not add a shield to a two-handed or ranged setup. Preserve the character's
identity, clothing, colors, handedness and equipment across the pair and all its
weapon sets. Treat conflicting descriptions/equipment as a question to resolve,
not permission to invent a different setup.

HUMANOID HEIGHT: Each combat square on the catalog's fixed 3 x 3 grid is 5 feet
by 5 feet. The catalog's humanoid_size is a visual body-height tier, separate
from sprite_size (combat squares) and from the SRD creature-type tag. "small"
humanoids stand 2-4 feet tall (0.4-0.8 square), according to their species and
the saved height_feet art target when present. Goblins are typically 3-4 feet;
kobolds are typically 2-3 feet. A quickling's saved height is an art-direction
estimate, not an exact current-SRD measurement. "normal" human-scale figures,
including orcs, should stand a full 5 feet tall (one square), not 3-4 feet.
"tall" figures, such as ogres and hill
giants, should read taller than normal figures; aim for about 1.7 squares when
their saved footprint permits. "na" means this humanoid-height comparison does
not apply to the body plan. Compare with correctly scaled art from the same
species and tier before choosing a body scale. Measure the figure from planted
feet to the top of its head, excluding weapons, horns, raised arms and effects.
For a small humanoid, use the individual record's height_feet, not a generic
Small-tier midpoint. Read height_reference to distinguish a published species
range from a chosen art target or an estimate.
Keep that body height consistent across both poses and every weapon set for this
creature; do not fill the 128 x 128 canvas independently for each sprite. Use
only a correctly scaled comparison sprite as a reference: an older accepted
image is not a height authority if it makes normal orcs 3-4 feet tall. These
are body measurements, not canvas sizes. Never shrink a normal body below the
5-foot target merely to make room for its weapon.

FOOTPRINT BOUNDARY: The saved sprite_size is the creature's game-derived tile
footprint, distinct from the 128 x 128 PNG canvas. In READY, a raised weapon
may extend above the footprint into the combat square above. It may render over
the combat sprite occupying that square. The ready weapon must stay within the
footprint's side and bottom boundaries. In ACTION, the moving attack weapon may
cross the dotted footprint boundary during its swing or thrust. In BOTH poses,
keep the body, planted feet, shield, and non-attacking gear inside the footprint.
This permitted weapon overflow is not a reason to shrink or recenter the figure.
The weapon must still be visible inside the PNG canvas with transparent margin;
pixels cannot be drawn outside the PNG. The grid never moves between monsters
or frames. If the body cannot fit at the correct height, resolve that conflict
before generating; never solve it by shrinking only one frame.

LEADER COMPARISON: For every creature named Leader or Ldr, compare its body
against regular creatures of the same kind in the same game archive. Make the
leader broader and more impressive in both poses and every weapon set, with
richer treatment of its saved armor and weapons. A 1 x 1 Normal leader and its
regular counterpart both have 5-foot bodies: do not shrink the regular figure
or push the leader's head above the square. Where the saved tier and footprint
leave room, the leader may also be modestly taller. Keep the game-derived
sprite_size unchanged and do not invent gameplay gear to signal rank.

WEAPON-SET COVERAGE (generation workflow): Inspect the monster's entire current
equipment list and enumerate every weapon set, including weapons not currently
readied. Each set needs its own ready.png AND action.png. A one-handed weapon
with an available shield is one unit; without a shield it is a weapon-only set.
Each ranged weapon and each two-handed weapon is a separate set. Bow + mace +
shield therefore needs a bow pair and a mace/shield pair. Bow + greatsword needs
a bow pair and a greatsword pair. Ask the user if a set or equipment classification
is unclear. This image request depicts ONLY its selected set and specified pose;
create the other sets and poses in separate requests, not a combined sheet.

PAIR REFERENCES: The approved original pose guide controls camera and pose.
The approved new ready sprite controls this monster's identity, palette, scale
and foot baseline when generating action. For another weapon set, also reference
this monster's first approved NEW ready sprite to retain identity. Reference
images must be supplied with the request, not merely named in its text.

SHARED FRAME BUDGET: Plan BOTH poses before drawing either one. Identify the
pose with the widest or tallest full-body-and-weapon silhouette, reserve a
transparent margin around that larger pose in the PNG canvas, and use its
camera distance for both images. The character's head, torso, limbs and
equipment keep the same apparent pixel scale in ready and action. Do not
shrink the whole action figure to fit a wider attack, and do not enlarge the
ready figure to fill spare space. The raised ready weapon may cross the top
tile-footprint line, and the moving action weapon may cross a tile-footprint
line, while all pixels remain inside the PNG. Do not reduce the required
humanoid body height to make room for the attack.

FIXED ANIMATION REGISTRATION: These are successive frames of ONE character,
not separately composed illustrations. Establish the ready figure's planted
feet and ground contact at fixed x AND y pixel coordinates on the 128 x 128
canvas. Make the action from the ready image at the SAME canvas origin: retain
the planted feet at those exact coordinates and keep the hips and torso largely
registered. Move only the limbs, weapon, clothing and modest upper-body lean
needed to show the attack. Do not translate, recenter, crop, independently
scale, or rotate the entire figure between frames. A matching foot baseline
alone is insufficient if the character slides horizontally. For a stationary
attack, do not invent a step or a new stance. Plan transparent space for the
weapon's widest action sweep before making ready; a raised ready weapon may
cross the top tile line, and the moving action weapon may cross a tile line.
If the weapon cannot fit inside the PNG,
revise the pose without changing body scale or foot anchor. Overlay the frames
and play them at combat size to reject any jump.

TORSO CONTINUITY: Keep a single connected figure from neck through chest,
abdomen, waist and hips in BOTH frames. Derive action from the complete ready
figure; do not paste an independently generated upper half onto a lower half
at a straight horizontal boundary. When changing an attacking arm, preserve
the torso and blend its shoulder into the original anatomy and clothing. Reject
any visible shear, seam, gap, doubled edge, abrupt garment-pattern break or
upper body that appears detached from the pelvis. Inspect the final exported
action enlarged around the torso and at normal combat size, then alternate it
with ready. Registered feet alone do not establish anatomical continuity.

OUTPUT: A single full-body figure, genuine transparent background, no painted
checkerboard, ground plane, labels, borders, scenery, shadows extending off the
figure or motion blur. Keep all visible pixels inside the PNG canvas with
transparent margin. A raised READY weapon may cross the top tile line; the
moving ACTION weapon may cross a footprint line. Final assets are 128 x 128
PNGs with alpha. Larger working images are allowed; export with nearest-neighbor
sampling and preserve alpha.
The final ready and action cutouts must have no opaque black boxes, wedges,
halos, or stray pixels; inspect both on light, dark, and contrasting backgrounds.
For a sword, preserve its recognizable blade length and continuous hand-to-tip
shape in BOTH frames at combat size; do not shorten it to fit a pose.
Keep camera, character scale and exact planted-foot coordinates consistent across poses
and weapon sets; do not resize each pose independently to fill the canvas. The
action must read clearly at normal combat size, not only in a magnified preview.
